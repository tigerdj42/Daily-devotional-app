1The book of the genealogy of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham. 2Abraham became the father of Isaac. Isaac became the father of Jacob. Jacob became the father of Judah and his brothers. 3Judah became the father of Perez and Zerah by Tamar. Perez became the father of Hezron. Hezron became the father of Ram. 4Ram became the father of Amminadab. Amminadab became the father of Nahshon. Nahshon became the father of Salmon. 5Salmon became the father of Boaz by Rahab. Boaz became the father of Obed by Ruth. Obed became the father of Jesse. 6Jesse became the father of King David. David became the father of Solomon by her who had been Uriah's wife. verses 7–17 · genealogy continues… 16Jacob became the father of Joseph, the husband of Mary, from whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ. 17So all the generations from Abraham to David are fourteen generations; from David to the exile to Babylon fourteen generations; and from the carrying away to Babylon to the Christ, fourteen generations. 18Now the birth of Jesus Christ was like this: After his mother, Mary, was engaged to Joseph, before they came together, she was found pregnant by the Holy Spirit. 19Joseph, her husband, being a righteous man, and not willing to make her a public example, intended to put her away secretly. 20But when he thought about these things, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream, saying, "Joseph, son of David, don't be afraid to take to yourself Mary, your wife, for that which is conceived in her is of the Holy Spirit. 21She shall give birth to a son. You shall call his name Jesus, for it is he who shall save his people from their sins." 22Now all this happened that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the Lord through the prophet, saying, 23"Behold, the virgin shall be with child, and shall give birth to a son. They shall call his name Immanuel"; which is, being interpreted, "God with us." 24Joseph arose from his sleep, and did as the angel of the Lord commanded him, and took his wife to himself; 25and didn't know her sexually until she had given birth to her firstborn son. He named him Jesus.

Matthew · Chapter 2
1Now when Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judea in the days of King Herod, behold, wise men from the east came to Jerusalem, saying, 2"Where is he who is born King of the Jews? When you have found him, bring me word, so that I also may come and worship him." 9They, having heard the king, went their way; and behold, the star, which they saw in the east, went before them until it came and stood over where the young child was. 10When they saw the star, they rejoiced with exceedingly great joy. 11They came into the house and saw the young child with Mary, his mother, and they fell down and worshiped him. Opening their treasures, they offered to him gifts: gold, frankincense, and myrrh. 12Being warned in a dream that they shouldn't return to Herod, they went back to their own country another way. 13Now when they had departed, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to Joseph in a dream, saying, "Arise and take the young child and his mother, and flee into Egypt, and stay there until I tell you, for Herod will seek the young child to destroy him." 14He arose and took the young child and his mother by night, and departed into Egypt, 15and was there until the death of Herod; that it might be fulfilled which was spoken by the Lord through the prophet, saying, "Out of Egypt I called my son." verses 16–18 · the massacre of the innocents… 19But when Herod was dead, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared in a dream to Joseph in Egypt, saying, 20"Arise and take the young child and his mother, and go into the land of Israel, for those who sought the young child's life are dead." 21He arose and took the young child and his mother, and came into the land of Israel. 22But when he heard that Archelaus was reigning over Judea in the place of his father, Herod, he was afraid to go there. Reflection
What's happening
Chapter 1 opens Matthew's Gospel with a genealogy tracing Jesus' lineage from Abraham through David to Joseph, establishing his rightful place in Israel's story. Matthew then recounts the announcement of Jesus' conception by the Holy Spirit to a troubled Joseph, who chooses faithfulness over reputation. Chapter 2 shifts to the arrival of Magi from the east, whose worship of the newborn king triggers Herod's jealous rage. Through divine warnings and dreams, the holy family flees to Egypt, fulfilling ancient prophecy, before returning safely to settle in Nazareth after Herod's death.
Key verse
"She shall give birth to a son. You shall call his name Jesus, for it is he who shall save his people from their sins."
Matthew 1:21 · WEB
Commentary
Matthew begins not with drama but with a list of names — a bold choice that grounds Jesus firmly in the sweep of Israel's history. As William Barclay observed, this genealogy was Matthew's way of saying to his Jewish audience: this is the One your whole story has been moving toward. The name "Jesus" — Yeshua — means "God saves," and Matthew wants us to feel the weight of that claim before we read another word. N.T. Wright notes that Joseph's quiet act of obedience is itself a kind of foreshadowing: the whole Gospel will ask ordinary people to receive extraordinary news and act on it. The Magi in chapter 2 are a striking counterpoint — foreign Gentiles who worship the Jewish Messiah while Jerusalem's own king plots his murder. Craig Keener points out that Matthew is already signalling what Jesus will be: saviour not only of Israel but of the nations, welcomed by unexpected outsiders, rejected by those who should have known him best.
A prayer for today
Lord Jesus, as I begin this journey through your Word, open my eyes to see you in every chapter — not as a distant figure from history, but as the living Saviour Matthew introduces. Thank you that your story reaches all the way back through generations of broken, ordinary people, and that you chose to enter our world through that very brokenness. Like Joseph, give me the courage to trust your word even when it asks something difficult of me. And like the Magi, let me seek you with everything I have, and bow in wonder when I find you. Amen.
